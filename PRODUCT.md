# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students and young professionals — primarily non-native English speakers preparing for IELTS, GRE, or similar high-stakes exams — who want to improve vocabulary, reading comprehension, and writing fluency by engaging with real, premium journalism (The Economist, The New Yorker, New Scientist). They use this daily during study sessions, in quiet focused contexts. They are goal-oriented: they track streaks, review flashcards, and want to see progress over time.

## Product Purpose

Reader & Writer is a curated reading and language-learning app. It surfaces premium magazine articles, lets users highlight and save excerpts, builds a personal Vocab Bank, and provides spaced-repetition exercises and flashcards. Success looks like: a user finishing their exam with a vocabulary and reading confidence they built one article at a time.

## Brand Personality

Clean, minimal, quietly confident. The app earns trust through restraint — it doesn't shout. Every feature feels deliberate. Think of a well-designed pocket notebook meeting a precision reading tool.

Three words: **clean · confident · focused**.

## Anti-references

- **Duolingo**: No cartoonish illustrations, no streak-pressure game loops, no loud color-block celebration screens, no mascot-driven UI. Encouragement should feel earned and quiet, not manufactured.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards**: No hero-metric templates (big number + small label + gradient accent). No blue-dominance for its own sake. Stats should support progress, not become the product.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is primary.** Typography, spacing, and color serve the articles and words — never compete with them.
2. **Progress is felt, not announced.** Streaks, mastery rings, and stats exist, but they live in context; no confetti, no popups demanding attention.
3. **Precision over decoration.** Each UI choice has a reason. Ornamental flourishes (gradients, animations, illustrations) are used only when they materially improve the experience.
4. **Calm confidence.** The interface should feel like a tool a thoughtful person built for themselves — controlled, direct, no hedging.
5. **Reward depth, not speed.** Readings are long-form. Layout and density should invite lingering, not encourage skimming.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Reduced-motion alternatives required for all animations. Color is never the sole conveyor of meaning (mastery levels use labels + color). Support right-to-left reading considerations for users whose native scripts differ.
