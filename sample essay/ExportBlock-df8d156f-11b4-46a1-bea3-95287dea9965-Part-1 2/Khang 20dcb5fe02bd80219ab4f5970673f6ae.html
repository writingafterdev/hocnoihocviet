Many believe that the government should be responsible for transporting children to school, while others think parents are more suitable. Personally, I disagree with the former view because the government may not ensure safety to all students during the school commutes.

On the one hand, one main reason why state-funded are preferred rather than parental transportation is that an increasing number of parents are currently unable to transport their children regularly to school. In today’s fast-paced world, many parents are often occupied with hectic schedules at work, which limits their time dedicated to  other necessary tasks, including transporting their children to school. To address this issue, they opt to transport services organized by the government, using state-supported buses or public metro systems. They believe this method is effective because public transport generally operates on fixed schedules, which can ensure children are transported to school daily with minimal delays.

However, others disagree with the above argument for several reasons. Specifically, as the government needs to manage a large number of students, it may struggle to provide adequate supervision, potentially resulting in risky situations such as a student being forgotten and left behind. There have been unfortunate cases illustrating this explanation, where some students are locked for hours in school buses without anyone noticing, causing serious health risks due to lack of oxygen. Additionally, the transportation services provided by the government are often standardized for the majority of students, potentially overlooking those with special or unexpected requirements. For instance, students with physical disabilities may require vehicles equipped with ramps or lifts, which are not always available in public transport services. These mistakes highlight the possible limitations of the one-size-fits-all services and underscore the need for tailored solutions to ensure all students can go to school safely and conveniently.

After considering those reasons,  I also believe that parents should transport their children rather than letting the government do it. One key factor is the level of personalized care that parents can offer. Unlike government-run transport services, which must cater to large groups of students and adhere to a strict schedule, parents can ensure their child’s safety and comfort by choosing the safest routes and adjusting travel times based on their child’s needs. Furthermore, parents are more familiar with any special requirements their child might have, whether it’s medical conditions, allergies, or behavioral needs that may not be immediately obvious to a bus driver or transportation officer.

In conclusion, although entrusting the government to transport students to school has certain merits, it could pose risks that lead to more serious consequences. Therefore, I believe that it is safer for children to go to school with their parents rather than depending on the services provided by the government.

Access to clean water is a basic human right. Therefore every home should have a water supply that is provided free of charge. Do you agree or disagree?

Many people contend that access to clean water for everyone should be totally free, because it is a basic human right. While this might be reasonable for various reasons, I disagree due to its scarcity and the cost of producing it.

On the one hand, it is morally understandable why some think clean water should be supplied to everyone free of charge. Firstly, it serves as an irreplaceable material for us to maintain daily essential demands, ranging from small-scale activities such as washing clothes and cooking food, to large-scale endeavors such as industrial and agricultural production. Secondly, in terms of medical applications, clean water is widely utilized for various purposes, particularly infusion therapy, which is a useful method to ensure patients stay hydrated during medical treatment. In contrast, consuming contaminated water in the long-term instead of clean water necessarily contributes to higher rates of malnutrition or even serious diseases such as diarrhea, all of which frequently appear in underdeveloped countries.

However, providing clean water should not be free at all, due to its scarcity and the cost of producing it is prohibitively high.  As for the first category, it has been shown that clean water sources are disappearing rapidly as a result of climate changes. For example, one of the largest sources of fresh water, Antarctic glaciers, are evaporating day by day because of global warming.  Regarding production cost, it requires high investment to manufacture clean water, from conducting researches for the appropriate source of water to building specialised factories for filtration, before making it accessible to every household through constructed pipe systems. Therefore, it is better not to utilize clean water in a wasteful way, and one way to ensure that is not providing it freely. Residents can exchange clean water with different means, whether they are cash, substitute goods, or even workforce.

To conclude, as clean water is a valuable resource for humans, we should utilize it moderately by not supplying it free of charge
