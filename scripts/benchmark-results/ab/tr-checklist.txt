# Step 1 — walk every hard requirement, in order

Take hardRequirements one at a time. Do not skip any, and do not merge two into
one judgement. For each requirement:

  a. Write down privately the requirement's question and its successTest.
  b. Search the essay for the text that answers it. Quote the strongest sentence
     you find, or note that you found none.
  c. Compare what you found against the successTest. Decide one of:
       ANSWERED  — the essay meets the successTest.
       PARTIAL   — the essay gestures at it but does not meet the successTest.
       ABSENT    — nothing in the essay addresses it.
  d. Report a finding for every PARTIAL and every ABSENT. Name the requirement
     in your own words, then say what the essay does instead. For PARTIAL quote
     the sentence that falls short; for ABSENT quote the sentence where the
     reader would have expected it — usually the introduction, the thesis, or
     the paragraph nearest the topic.

A task with four hard requirements and three unmet produces three findings. Two
requirements missed in the same paragraph are two findings, because the writer
has to fix both.

# Step 2 — the burdens the writer created

Now take conditionalRequirements. Each has a trigger. Read the essay for that
trigger. If the writer made the triggering claim, ask the diagnosticQuestion and
report a finding when the essay does not answer it. If the writer never made the
claim, that requirement does not apply — say nothing about it.

This is where a writer's own argument creates work: naming an actor who should
act, asserting a cause, or claiming one side outweighs another. The prompt does
not demand these; the writer's sentence does.

# Step 3 — position and consistency

State privately the position the introduction commits to. Then check the
conclusion states the same position, and that no body paragraph argues against
it without marking the concession. Report each mismatch, quoting both ends.

# What a finding must contain

Name the specific thing missing: the requirement not met, the step skipped, the
detail that would have made it concrete. One plain sentence, no hedging.

Reasoning is enough — never demand statistics, named studies or real events.
Never require both sides unless the task asks for both sides. If the essay
answers a requirement by a route the profile did not anticipate, judge that
route on its own logic and report nothing.

optionalAngles are possible routes, never obligations. An essay that skips every
optional angle and still meets each successTest has no Task Response finding
from Step 1.

# Severity

Set severity from the checklist, not from feel. An ABSENT hard requirement is
"major". A PARTIAL hard requirement is "moderate". An unmet conditional burden
is "moderate". A position inconsistency is "major". Anything else is "minor".
